5 reasons your website gets no traffic from Google

These are the problems I find most often when a site “does everything right” and still doesn't grow. Each one takes a few minutes to check in Google Search Console — no tools to buy.

1 Google doesn't index your important pages

Sign
Service, product or category pages aren't in Google at all — so they can't rank for anything.
Where to look
Search Console → Pages. Open “Crawled – currently not indexed” and “Discovered – currently not indexed”. If your key pages are there, this is problem #1.
What it means
Google saw the page and decided it isn't worth showing: too similar to others, too thin, or too weakly linked inside the site. “Request indexing” won't change that decision.
Fix first
Make each key page clearly different and useful, link to it from your main menu or category pages, and remove near-duplicate pages (tags, filters, test pages).

2 There's no page for what people actually search

Sign
Your homepage shows up for everything, and nothing ranks well.
Where to look
Search Console → Performance → Queries, then click a query and switch to Pages. If the homepage answers queries about specific services, products or cities, you're missing landing pages.
Fix first
One page per service, category or location people search for — with its own title, content and internal links. Structure first, design second.

3 Something technical blocks Google

Sign
Traffic dropped right after a redesign, a migration or a new website launch.
Where to look
Search Console → URL Inspection for a key page: is it “Indexing allowed”? Which canonical did Google choose? Then check that robots.txt doesn't block important sections and no noindex was left over from development.
Fix first
Remove the block, make canonicals point to the page itself, and make sure the main content is visible in the HTML Google renders.

4 Your pages are stuck on page 2

Sign
Plenty of impressions, almost no clicks.
Where to look
Search Console → Performance → Pages, turn on “Average position” and filter positions between 8 and 20. These pages are relevant in Google's eyes — just not the best yet.
Fix first
Before rewriting anything: link to these pages from stronger pages of your site, and make the title answer the search intent. It's the cheapest growth you have.

5 The wrong version shows up in the wrong country

Sign
You sell in several countries or languages, and some versions get no traffic at all.
Where to look
Search Console → Performance → Countries, then filter by page. If people in Germany land on your English pages (or no version ranks in a market), the international setup is broken.
Fix first
Check that every language version has correct hreflang links back and forth, its own canonical, and content written for that market — not just translated.
